Still in pain after the crash, but the X-rays came back clean?

That gap between what you feel and what the scans show is not in your head. It is the single most common place injury claims lose value.

What you are describing fits a soft tissue injury pattern. The pain is real. The problem is what the insurer's file says about it, and that part can be checked.

Why the file says less than you feel

Soft tissue injuries like whiplash often do not show up on X-rays. Insurers know this, and claim files get valued on what imaging and paperwork can show. When the scan is clean, the injury gets treated as minor, whatever your days actually feel like.

That gap is where these claims get discounted. Not because the pain is not real, but because nobody made the file prove it. Treatment records, specialist notes, and documented time off work are what close that gap, and most first offers arrive before any of it is counted.

From one reviewed case

One driver with a soft tissue injury was first offered $4,200. After treatment records, specialist notes, and lost work were fully counted, the claim ended near $47,500.
